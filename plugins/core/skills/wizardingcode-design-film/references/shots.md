# The shot

A film that plays under a scroll is one shot, designed before it is generated. These rules say which shots hold up when a visitor drags them forward and back, and which waste an attempt.

## The rules

1. The film moves the way the page moves. A scroll downward reads as going down, going in, opening or arriving. A subject that rises while the page goes down works against the hand that moves it.
2. A single subject in a single unbroken move, with no cut. A cut under a scroll is a jump the visitor did not ask for. Do not ask for one thing to become another: where the brief insists, hide the change inside a flash or a cloud and keep the heading, the place in the frame and the speed the same on both sides.
3. The path is fixed and the subject stays alive. The route through the frame does not wander; the thing on it ripples, sways, adjusts, and the scene around it breathes (steam, drifting light, moving cloud). A subject frozen on a clean path reads as a still that slides.
4. The ending is written first. The last frame is where the page rests, so describe it as an arrival: the place of each thing and the state of the light. Leave margin above and below the subject, since the page's header covers the frame's top edge and a wider or shorter screen crops its sides. A texture that fills the frame, with nothing to crop, is safe on every screen.
5. Choose subjects that forgive. Fluid, mist, steam, light, fabric and things seen from a distance come out well. A thing whose exact form every viewer knows from close (a hand, a keyboard, a familiar animal) shows every error; keep it far, simple and moving.
6. Where two concepts are equal, take the one that moves along the vertical axis. It matches the scroll one to one in both directions.
7. Compose for the page. Decide where in the frame the action runs and leave a calm region for the words. Describe that region as part of the scene (soft shadow, depth that recedes, a plain surface), never as emptiness or darkness: a tool asked for empty dark edges paints black panels. For a subject in the centre, say it stands exactly midway between the two side edges, and that both sides hold the same quiet.
8. A crossing is written with what the lens does. Where the camera passes through water, mist or glass, name the splash, the drops on the lens, the instant out of focus. A crossing with no trace on the lens looks made.
9. A product is the client's own or it is close. An object with no mark, far away, reads as a placeholder. Land near enough that its form carries it, or use the client's own photograph as the first frame.
10. No text and no logo, in every prompt. A tool that is asked for a sign paints one, and paints it wrong.

## The frame's form

A prompt for the first frame answers these lines, each from the rule it names. Write the answers as plain sentences, in the brand's own terms.

```
Which way the film will travel (rule 1): [down, inward, opening or arriving], so this frame is [what is seen before that starts].
The one subject (rules 2, 5 and 9): [what it is, what it is made of, how far it stands from the camera].
Room for its path (rule 3): [where it will go from here, and that the frame leaves that way clear].
Where the words will stand (rule 7): [the calm part of the scene, named as a thing in it, and its side of the frame].
The look: [the light and the colours of this brand's world].
Refused (rule 10): No text and no logo in the picture.
```

## The film's form

A prompt for the film answers these lines, in this order.

```
Refused (rule 10): No text, no logo and no lettering at any moment of the clip.
Unbroken (rule 2): [the single move, in one take, with nothing turning into something else].
Way of travel (rules 1 and 6): [which way the picture goes as the page scrolls down, and along which axis].
What holds and what lives (rule 3): [the route, which does not wander; what on the subject keeps moving; what in the scene keeps moving].
A surface crossed (rule 8): [what is left on the lens, or leave this line out].
The arrival (rule 4): [the last frame: the place of each thing, the light, the margin above and below].
```

Six seconds at 1080p is the starting point: the clip is encoded again for the web, and a larger frame only costs more.

## A journey longer than one clip

Each clip is approved by itself before the next is made.

- The next clip starts from the last frame of the approved one, taken at full quality with the command in `encode.md`. A frame saved for review carries its compression into everything after it.
- The next prompt continues the motion: the same heading, the same speed, the same light. Where a clip ends on a frame that is nearly empty, say what grows out of it.
- Only the last clip ends at rest. The ones before it end in motion.
- A join falls where something is moving, or where the picture has a reason to change its texture: something crossing the lens, a loss of focus, a beat of dark. Fine texture is drawn again by each generation, so two rests put end to end show as a cut.
- Worlds made of light, particles and air join most reliably: they hold nothing a continuation can draw wrong.
