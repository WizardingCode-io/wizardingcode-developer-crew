# Reviewing motion

For the reviewer of a built page, and for the session before it hands the page over. The page is open in a browser; the thesis is beside it.

## Before looking

1. Read the thesis: the moves, and the list of what stays still. A page with no thesis is returned before any move is judged.
2. List what moves on the page, by device. Count kinds, then elements: a device repeated unchanged down the page is one kind.
3. For each surface, say how often a person meets it. The answer sets what the surface may carry.

## The questions, in order

Ask each of every move. The first that fails is the finding; the later ones wait for the fix.

1. **Purpose.** Which of the gate's six does it serve? A move that serves none is deleted.
2. **Frequency and the keyboard.** Is it on a thing met many times a day, or started by a key? Then it is feedback at most.
3. **Curve.** Does it start at once and settle, and never gather speed into the person's eye?
4. **Length.** Is it inside the standard's row for its kind, and is its exit shorter than its entrance?
5. **Origin and path.** Does it start at what caused it, at a size near its own, and leave by the path it came?
6. **Interruption.** Fired twice fast, does it turn from where it is? Can every visible control be pressed while it runs?
7. **Cost.** Does it change transform and opacity alone, or is what else it changes named in the thesis and measured?
8. **Reduced motion.** With the setting on, does it keep its opacity and lose its travel? Switched with the page open, does the next move obey?
9. **No script, no stylesheet.** Is every word of the page readable with scripts blocked?
10. **Belonging.** Does it share its family of timing with the rest of its component, and its character with the rest of the page?

## How to look

- Slow the move down in the browser's animation inspector and step through it. Ask of the frames: do two states show at once; does the start or the stop jerk; is the origin where the cause is; do the parts of a group move as one gesture.
- Record the screen and watch the recording. A group that felt fluid is often, on the recording, items waiting in line.
- Come back to it a day later. A move that pleased its author on the day it was written is the one most often cut.

## The form of a finding

One finding a move, with five parts:

- **Where**: the file and the line, and the device by its name.
- **Question**: the number of the question it failed.
- **Now**: what it does, with its present values.
- **Instead**: the remedy, by the order of remedies in `SKILL.md`, with the standard's value written out.
- **Why**: one sentence that names what the person sees.

Findings are ordered by what the person feels first: a move that blocks or misleads; a move that should not exist; cost and dropped frames; interruption and timing; origin and belonging; reduced motion.

## What a reviewer does not decide from code

Whether a cross-fade reads as one thing. Whether a spring's settle feels right. Whether a stagger reads as one gesture. Whether the page's motion reads as made by hand. For each, the finding names the moment to look at and leaves the judgement to a person who looks.
