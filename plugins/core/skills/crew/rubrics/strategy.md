# Strategy rubric

The strategy of a new project (SP7 §7.3), read against `conclave/researcher.md`. Five questions; a "no" on a line of `strategy.md` is a defect, and the ruling is not proven while one stands.

1. **Proportion.** Is every element the Stack or the design proposes required by a need the Viability section states with a source? An element nobody asked for and no fact calls for is over-engineering: named with its line, a defect.
2. **Proof.** Does every claim's mark `[R<n>]` point at a fact line that says what the claim says? A mark on a fact that says something else, or a claim that stretches its fact, is a defect.
3. **Measured.** Are the Cost and the Time figures given with a basis (a fact, a count, a comparable), never a guess? A figure with no basis is a defect; a range with its basis is fine.
4. **Phases.** Does every scaling row name a trigger somebody could observe (a number of users, requests, records, a date), the change it brings and its cost? A trigger nobody could observe, or a change with no cost, is a defect.
5. **Persuasion.** Where the request asked for more than the need, is the smaller answer argued point by point against the request's reasons? A smaller answer asserted without the argument is a defect; a larger answer taken because the user asked, with no fact, is a defect.

Advisory: wording, order, a claim repeated, a figure that could be sharper. Outside the strategy (a line of the research, of the brief): advisory, named as such.
