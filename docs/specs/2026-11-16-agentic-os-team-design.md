## 1. Why

The engine has an `agentic-os` team (`packages/engine/src/team.ts`, `TEAMS["agentic-os"]`) with the lead `lead-agentic-os`, no member, no gate, a seat by paths and `validates: false`, and a pack, `packs/agentic-os`, with three checklists and one knowledge file. Nothing in the engine or the plugin says where that team reads about what it builds, which tools it runs, or which MCP servers and skills it uses.

### What the team is

A team like the others: it works inside the crew's method, on the crew's items, with the crew's gates. Its trade is building agentic bots and agentic features inside our projects, on any stack. The user's words of 2026-11-16:

- What it is for: "esta equipa tem que estar sempre actualizada com as novas funcionalidade de agents skills mcp e approaches agenticos, conhecimento, treino memoria e melhoria"; "idependentemente da stack ou framework tem que ser capaz de desenvolver fluxos agentic solutions com uma precisao nivel openbot ou grokbot ou outro qualquer que oriente as tools fluxos workflows para que tudo trabalhe como um ecosistema dentro da app bem reponsivo proactivo e que realmente de resultados acima dos 90% de exatidao"; "tem que conseguir criar skills ou instalar skills nas soluçoes, sistema de memoria sistema de conhecimento e aprendizagem".
- What kind of team: "sabes que esta equipa é para desenvolver agentic bots ou os nos nossos projectos certo esta equipa funcionar como qualuqer outra equipa que temos mas neste contexto esta equipa é a que tem expertise para desenvolver isto".

So the team builds assistants that route tools, flows and workflows inside a client's application, responsive and proactive, with a skills system, a memory system and a knowledge and learning system, and it keeps itself current with what is new in agents, skills and MCP.

Two things are kept apart, and the user confirmed the split on 2026-11-16 with the words "Sim, mas B mais aberto":

- A. What the team builds into the client's product: memory, vector stores, observability, and the product's own MCP servers and skills. These are components of the product, chosen in section 4, under the product's own access control. The rule on servers does not limit them.
- B. What the crew's agent connects to while it works. The standing rule of the other definitions holds: servers with a reading mode, the documentation servers and the MCP Inspector. For this team alone it is more open: in the project's own local or test environment the agent may also connect to the servers of the components it is building (memory, vector store, observability and evaluation, the last added by the user's decision of 2026-11-16 on promptfoo's server), even where they write, because testing the write is part of the work, and never against the client's real data.

### What "above 90%" means

The user chose "the right decision, repeated", the option recommended. Per project: a set of the client's real cases; a closed set of tools and flows; the assistant choosing the right tool or flow in more than 90% of the cases; and each case run several times and counted right only if right every time. The task's final outcome is measured apart. The team never says "accuracy" without those four things.

### The public figures

No public benchmark shows 90% on an open domain, and no named product publishes the rate at which it chooses the right tool: the research found none for Claude Code, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Cursor, Grok Bot, any OpenBot or OpenClaw. What is published, each figure said as its publisher's own or as independent:

- The tau-bench family, Sierra's public boards, read at https://taubench.com/ with undated entries: the best score on tau2-bench is 87.9%, and on tau3-Banking 55.2%, both at pass^1, one run a case. The site does not define pass^1, and the research found no pass^k value on the current boards.
- The fall over repeated runs, from the original tau-bench paper as a secondary source quotes it: GPT-4o on the retail domain went from 61.2% at pass^1 to under 25% at pass^8. The 61.2% rests on a search result, the page being https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/tau-bench-explained (not opened); the fall below 25% at pass^8 in retail is also in the research's reading of the paper's own page (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045).
- The Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard, at V4, last updated 2026-04-12 (https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html): the page opened and its table did not load, so no score was read there. Third-party aggregators disagree, at 75.0% and 76.7% for the leader, and one states 88.5% in a FAQ that is not reconciled with its own table; these rest on search results, the pages being https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/bfcl_v4/ (not opened) and https://benchlm.ai/md/benchmarks/bfcl-v4.md (not opened).
- Anthropic's own figures, on an internal evaluation that is not public (https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use, of 2025-11-24): with tool search, accuracy on its MCP evaluations went from 49% to 74% on Opus 4 and from 79.5% to 88.1% on Opus 4.5; examples on a tool's definition raised accuracy on complex parameters from 72% to 90%. The best of the first set is below 90%.
- Independent checks of tool search over very large catalogues, both resting on search results: Arcade reported 56% retrieval accuracy with regex and 64% with BM25 over 4,027 tools, its page being https://blog.arcade.dev/anthropic-tool-search-4000-tools-test (not opened); Stacklok, a competing vendor, reported 34% for Anthropic's tool against 94% for its own over 2,792 tools, its page being https://stacklok.com/blog/stackloks-mcp-optimizer-vs-anthropics-tool-search-tool-a-head-to-head-comparison/ (not opened), a competitor's claim.
- LangChain's own measurement of three architectures on a modified tau-bench with gpt-4o (https://www.langchain.com/blog/benchmarking-multi-agent-architectures, of 2025-06-10): a single agent falls off with two or more unrelated domains of tools added. The exact scores are in charts only.
- Customer-service products publish a resolution rate, a business outcome and not the choice of a tool. Intercom's own figure for Fin is a 76% average, "many seeing 85%+" by the research's note of the page (https://fin.ai/ai-engine, no date on the page); one independent test on four small-business clients gave 38%, which rests on a search result, the research naming for its secondary figures on Fin, without saying which figure is on which page, https://www.usefini.com/guides/good-ai-support-resolution-rate-vendors-tested (not opened) with two pages of Decagon's.
- Why repetition is in the measure, in Anthropic's words (https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents, published "Jan 9, 2026"): "pass^k measures the probability that all k trials succeed.", and "pass@k for tools where one success matters, pass^k for agents where consistency is essential." By the same post, a case that passes 75% of the time passes three runs in a row about 42% of the time.
- The size of a first set and the judge, same post: "20-50 simple tasks drawn from real failures is a great start"; regression evals "should have a nearly 100% pass rate"; "LLM-as-judge graders should be closely calibrated with human experts".

So "above 90%" is said of a set of the client's real cases, a closed set of tools and flows, the first decision counted apart from the task's outcome, and each case run several times and counted right only if right every time.
