# SP9: Frontend proven and with craft. Design

- Status: approved by the user on 2026-10-26 (the plan of this date), to be built as 0.16.0
- Supersedes nothing; extends the SP7 design §6.2 and §8.3 (the UI/UX gate and the e2e gate) and ADR 0010 (packs)

## 1. Why

The crew proves little of a frontend change today. The UI/UX reviewer reads captures the session took by hand in a browser it drives itself; without a browser the gate is recorded not run, and in the eval sandbox an interface change never ended `Proven:`. The rubric asks six questions and says nothing of motion, measured accessibility, performance or a design contract. The frontend team has no member, no gate and no validation. None of the design tools installed on the user's machine is read by the crew.

The user's direction of 2026-10-26: frontend work by the crew has to be "more than perfect" and dynamic (motion, interaction), and that comes from taking the best of sixteen outside sources into **our own** agents, skills, workflows and packs, never from invoking somebody else's plugin. The user's projects are Vue and Nuxt; other users of the plugin build with Laravel Blade or React and Next; JavaScript and TypeScript are the base.

## 2. Decisions of 2026-10-26

1. Scope: prove the frontend in the gate, implement with craft, and ship the knowledge as packs.
2. Stacks: JS/TS as the base; Vue/Nuxt first, Laravel Blade and React/Next beside it; Expo/React Native is a subproject of its own (a simulator, not a browser).
3. Proof: **both** the crew's Playwright fixture run by the project's own e2e during the e2e gate (the base: headless, nobody present, inside the sandbox) **and** the `chrome-devtools` CLI launched by the engine as a deep audit (Lighthouse, performance traces). The CLI runs only with Google's telemetry off, as a proposed command a person confirms; without it the report says `audit: not captured`, never a pass.
4. Adoption: the outside rules enter **distilled** into the core (the standard, the rubric, the reviewer, the method, a deterministic gate in the engine) and into the packs (checklists, knowledge), with provenance (source, licence, commit) as ADR 0010 asks; conflicts resolved into one value per rule; no outside plugin invoked, no closed binary run.
5. Shape: one release, 0.16.0: the core, `packs/frontend` (team), `packs/nuxt` (stack), `packs/next` (stack) and the Blade/Livewire/Dusk additions to `packs/laravel`.

## 4. The evidence is the engine's (D1)

The crew's Playwright fixture, `plugins/core/e2e/playwright.mjs` (one ESM file, the project's own `@playwright/test` resolved from the working directory), gains two helpers the project's e2e specs call:

- `capture(page, pageName, state, options)`: per viewport of the plan, a screenshot in light and, when the project has a dark scheme, in dark; the console entries since the previous capture; the accessibility snapshot (`body.ariaSnapshot()`); axe when `@axe-core/playwright` resolves (violations only), otherwise `not captured` with the reason; web vitals from in-page observers (CLS, LCP, an INP approximation, long tasks); a reduced-motion pass that lists the animations still running under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- `captureMotion(page, pageName, interaction, trigger, { reset?, windowMs })`: an in-page frame sampler, frames through CDP screencast (Chromium) or sequential screenshots, `document.getAnimations()` sampled at +0 and +100 ms (duration, delay, easing, iterations, fill, the keyframes' properties), frame statistics, and the reduced-motion rerun when `reset` is given. The frames stay on disk for the session; **one strip** of eight frames, composed in the page itself on a canvas, is what the reviewer reads.

The e2e gate passes two more variables beside today's two: `WIZARDINGCODE_CREW_UI_DIR` (`.crew/work/<id>/review/ui/`, absolute) and `WIZARDINGCODE_CREW_UI_PLAN` (`.crew/work/<id>/review/ui-plan.json`). Outside a gate run the helpers write nothing, so the project's CI runs the same spec.

**Files under `review/ui/`** (names `[a-z0-9-]+`; `@` for the width, never `:`): `<page>-<state>@<w>.png` and `<page>-<state>@<w>-dark.png`; `console-<page>-<state>.json` (a JSON array, the contract `listCaptures` reads today); `<page>-<state>.aria.yml`; `axe-<page>-<state>.json`; the capture's metadata `<page>-<state>.json`:

```json
{"page":"checkout","state":"error","url":"…","at":"…","by":"fixture",
 "viewports":[{"name":"mobile","width":360,"height":800,"light":"checkout-error@360.png","dark":null}],
 "console":"console-checkout-error.json","aria":"checkout-error.aria.yml",
 "axe":{"status":"clean|violations|not captured","reason":null,"file":"axe-checkout-error.json",
        "violations":[{"id":"color-contrast","impact":"serious","nodes":2,"targets":["button.pay"]}]},
 "vitals":{"cls":0.02,"lcpMs":820,"inpMs":null,"longTasks":0},
 "reducedMotion":{"status":"clean|running","running":[{"target":"div.spinner","name":"spin","iterations":"Infinity","durationMs":1000,"properties":["transform"]}]}}
```

and the motion sample `<page>-<interaction>.motion.json` with `motion/<page>-<interaction>/NN.png` and `<page>-<interaction>.strip.png`:

```json
{"page":"checkout","interaction":"open-summary","at":"…","windowMs":1000,
 "frames":{"method":"cdp-screencast|screenshots","dir":"motion/checkout-open-summary","strip":"checkout-open-summary.strip.png","count":8,"times":[0,16,33]},
 "frameTimes":{"samples":36,"meanMs":16.8,"p95Ms":21,"maxMs":48,"over50ms":0},
 "animations":[{"target":"aside.summary","kind":"CSSTransition","name":"transform","durationMs":250,"delayMs":0,"easing":"cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)","iterations":1,"fill":"none","properties":["transform","opacity"]}],
 "reducedMotion":{"status":"clean|running|not captured","reason":null,"animations":[]}}
```

**Viewports** come from the plan: `profile.ui.viewports` (`crew profile set ui.viewports "360x800,768x1024,1280x800"`), the three by default; schemes `light` and, when the project has a dark scheme, `dark`. The fixture bounds the images per item: the default state at the three widths in light plus dark at 1280; every other state at 360 and 1280; at most three pages per item (more pages become one todo per page) and six interactions.

**Screens are declared on the criteria.** `Criterion.screens?: { page, state }[]`, written by `crew criteria <id> add "…" --screen checkout:error,checkout:empty`. The engine derives the expected captures as the union of the live criteria's screens, writes `review/ui-plan.json` before the run (engine-owned: `review/ui/` is the session's, so what the engine protects lives beside it) and, after grading, `review/ui-evidence.json`: declared, captured and missing, per page, state, viewport, scheme and interaction, with the axe, vitals, reduced-motion, motion and audit summaries. The reviewer, the frontend lead and the report read that manifest; `crew gate run` prints `captures: 3 of 5 expected (fixture 3, session 0); missing: checkout-error, checkout-empty; viewports 360, 768, 1280; 1 without a console file: …`.

**The evidence is the e2e gate's third measurement**, after the console and the logs, not a new gate: the gate table of ADR 0002 stands, `allowSkips: ["e2e"]` covers it, and a project with no fixture and no declared screen behaves as today. `gradeUi(dir, plan)` in `packages/engine/src/ui.ts` grades in this order of offence: exit, console, logs, `ui: screen checkout-error declared by AC-2 not captured`, `ui: animation still running under reduced motion: div.spinner (checkout-default)`, `ui: axe serious color-contrast on checkout-error (2 nodes)`, `ui: CLS 0.18 on checkout-default@360 (limit 0.1)`. The thresholds are fixed in `ui.ts`: CLS 0.1; axe `serious` or `critical`; under reduced motion an animation with infinite iterations, or one moving a transform or layout property, or an opacity change longer than 300 ms. A forged evidence file can fail the gate and never pass it: the reviewer still rules. `GateRun.artifacts.ui` keeps the summary and the report gains a "UI evidence" section.

A project without Playwright keeps today's path: with a browser the session can drive, one capture per page, state and viewport as `<page>-<state>@<w>.png` with its console file, marked `by: "session"`; with neither, `crew judgement <id> record ui-ux --not-run --reason "no evidence"`.

## 5. The deterministic gates and the audit (D2, D3)

**`design-lint`** is a new built-in gate modelled on `secrets`: no command, planned at `.` when the diff touches an interface file or a design-contract path, run on a todo's gate as well. `packages/engine/src/design-lint.ts`, `lintInterfaceFiles(root, changed, { disabled, contract })`, text only. Rules and verdicts: `motion/transition-all` defect; `motion/scale-from-zero` defect; `motion/layout-property` defect (a transition or a `@keyframes` block on width, height, top, left, right, bottom, margin or padding); `motion/duration-over-500` defect on a literal (a `var()` or an identifier counts as `unresolved`, never judged); `motion/ease-in` advisory; `motion/reduced-motion-missing` defect (the diff adds an animation, a transition, `<Transition` or `motion.` and no file of the project names `prefers-reduced-motion`, `useReducedMotion`, `reducedMotion` or `motion-reduce:`); `hover/unguarded` advisory (`:hover` with no `(hover: hover)` in the same file; Tailwind's `hover:` never); `perf/will-change` advisory (more than two per file); `tokens/raw-colour` defect only when `DESIGN.md` declares colour tokens. The verdict line: `design-lint (.): fail in 0.1s`, `reason: motion/transition-all src/A.vue:12, +2 more`; the tail lists every hit as `<rule> <file>:<line>` and the advisories; `review/design-lint.json` (engine-owned) holds the hits and the **inventory of animated declarations** (file, line, property, duration, easing, kind) the reviewer reads. A false positive carries its rule id: `policy.json` `ui.lint.disabled: [...]` (named in the tail as `rules disabled by policy`), an inline `crew-lint: allow <rule> -- <why>` comment (counted), `crew skip` only when `allowSkips` lists the gate (not by default). What text cannot decide is the reviewer's: whether to animate at all, springs, computed durations, GSAP timelines, Blade and Livewire runtime classes.

**The deep audit** runs once per item through `packages/engine/src/devtools.ts`, after the e2e command and before the environment stops. Availability: `profile.browser.devtools` (a `GateCommand`); detection proposes `chrome-devtools` when it is on PATH and `findBrowser()` finds a Chrome; `crew profile set browser.devtools "<cmd>"` sets and confirms, `crew profile confirm browser` confirms the proposed one. Proposed and unconfirmed: `audit: not captured (chrome-devtools proposed; crew profile confirm browser)`; absent: `audit: not captured (no chrome-devtools on PATH: npm i -g chrome-devtools-mcp)`; no `environment.url`: `audit: not captured (no environment.url)`. Never a verdict by default. The commands run through `runCommand` with `CHROME_DEVTOOLS_MCP_NO_USAGE_STATISTICS=1`: `start --headless --isolated --no-usage-statistics --no-performance-crux`, `new_page --url`, `lighthouse_audit <id> --mode navigation --device mobile --outputDirPath review/ui/audit`, `performance_start_trace <id> --reload --autoStop --filePath review/ui/audit/trace-<n>.json`, `stop` in a `finally`; 60 s per command, 5 minutes in all, at most five pages (`environment.url` and the captures' URLs). Files: `review/ui/audit/lighthouse-<n>.json`, `trace-<n>.json`, `insights-<n>.txt`, and the engine's summary `audit.json` (scores, failed audits, LCP, CLS, INP). The audit fails the e2e run only on a Lighthouse accessibility audit at 0 with items, and on CLS above 0.1 when the fixture measured none; LCP, INP and the other scores are advisory lines (lab figures on a dev server).

## 6. The standard, the rubric, the roles and the method (D5, D6)

**The standard** lives in one file, `plugins/core/skills/crew/references/frontend.md` (about 6 KB), read by the session before the first interface file, by the reviewer after the rubric and by the frontend lead; the judge reads the rubric. One value per rule, the band the reviewer accepts in parentheses:

| Rule | Value |
| :- | :- |
| 100+ uses a day (press, row hover, focus, toggle, tab) | no animation, or feedback at 120 ms |
| Tens of uses a day (menu, tooltip, dropdown, accordion) | 200 ms at most, transform and opacity only |
| Occasional (modal, drawer, page or view change) | 300 ms at most, a drawer 400 |
| Rare, one authored moment per page | 800 ms at most, named in the motion thesis, once |
| Press | `scale(0.97)`, 120 ms (100 to 160) |
| Tooltip | 150 ms in, 100 ms out (125 to 200) |
| Dropdown, popover, menu | 200 in, 150 out, `transform-origin` at the trigger, from `scale(0.95)` and opacity 0 |
| Modal | 250 in, 200 out, `scale(0.96)` to 1 with opacity |
| Drawer, toast | 300 in, 250 out, a translate from its edge |
| Exit | shorter than its enter |
| Stagger | 40 ms per sibling, 200 ms in all, none past five siblings |
| Easing, enter and exit | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` |
| Easing, a move on screen | `ease-in-out` |
| Easing, hover colour | `ease`, 120 ms |
| Easing, constant motion | `linear` |
| Never | `ease-in` on UI; bounce or elastic without the thesis; `scale(0)`; `transition: all`; over 500 ms outside the thesis |
| Properties | `transform`, `opacity`; `clip-path`, `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `box-shadow` only when the thesis names them and the sample's frames stay under 16.7 ms; never width, height, top, left, margin, padding |
| Interruptible | transitions or springs for a reversible state; keyframes only one-shot |
| Reduced motion | opacity only, same duration; no parallax, scroll-driven or autoplay; never a blanket `transition: none` |
| Hover | behind `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; never the only way to see information |
| Frames | 16.7 ms per frame in the sample; more than two consecutive dropped frames is a defect |

Why 40 ms and not 100 for the stagger: both sources keep UI motion under 300 ms; at 100 ms a list of six lands its last item at 600 ms, past that ceiling; 40 ms times five keeps the list inside 200 ms. Why 0.97 and not 0.96 for the press: the press is the most frequent animation of the table, so it must be the least visible; on a 40 px control 0.97 is 1.2 px.

The other sections: the interface floor (the craft floor and the failure patterns; the states hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, error, empty and success; contrast 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and non-text; targets 24 px, 44 on touch; a focus ring from the tokens, never removed without a replacement; a body measure of 65 to 75 characters; icons from one set, never emoji; the refuse list: identical card grids, alternating left-right sections, eyebrows, gradient text, glass by default, side-stripe borders, counts or testimonials with no source, motion for spectacle, a page whose error and empty branches cannot be reached); accessibility by WCAG 2.2 criterion number (2.1.1, 2.4.3, 2.1.2, 2.4.7, 2.4.11, 1.3.1, 4.1.2, 1.4.3, 1.4.11, 1.4.10, 1.4.4, 2.5.8, 2.3.3); the states rule (every state the diff adds or changes is enumerated before any capture; a listed state with no evidence is a gap in the run, a reachable branch not listed is a gap in the spec, both named); copy (four tells: the triplet filler, the "not X, it's Y" contrast and the dash aside, a heading that announces instead of naming, an empty state that explains the product instead of giving the next action); responsive (360, 768 and 1280 for every changed page, 320 when the page has a form or a table, the dark scheme when the project has one); the design contract (`DESIGN.md` at the project root: YAML frontmatter with the normative tokens `colors`, `typography`, `spacing`, `rounded`, `shadows` and a `motion` block for the values the project overrides, then seven sections; without one the rubric's contract question is **Not answerable: no design contract**, never advisory; on the standard and full routes the gap round proposes one from the stylesheets, taken as an `observed` criterion of the item when the user accepts; nothing on the fast route). A provenance footer per section (source, licence, what entered, commit).

**The rubric**, `rubrics/ui-ux.md`, asks ten questions: 1 the criterion in the capture; 2 the declared states captured and the reachable branches the spec did not declare; 3 keyboard and focus from the accessibility snapshot and the `focus-visible` capture; 4 measured accessibility (axe serious or critical, contrast on the diff's text); 5 copy; 6 the design contract (a raw value where a token exists, a token used against its meaning, a colour outside the palette); 7 responsive at the mandatory widths and schemes; 8 motion against the standard (each sample against the table); 9 motion against the thesis (each animation has its line in the thesis; a 100+/day control animated past feedback; a rejected candidate built); 10 console and vitals. Defects: 1; 2 (a declared state absent, an undeclared reachable branch); 3 (unreachable, unnamed, focus not visible); 4; 5 (wrong language, raw error, placeholder text); 6; 7 (overflow, overlap or a hidden control at a mandatory width); 8 (any row of the table broken on an element the diff animates, the reduced-motion pass ignored included); 9; 10 (a console error or warning; CLS above the limit). Advisories: spacing and alignment that still read; a longer sentence; a loading state missing under a second; crowding that holds; a value inside the band but not the default; one sibling past the stagger cap; axe moderate or minor; INP and LCP figures; a tell that reads fine in context; a change beyond the stated intent. Not answerable: a declared state with no capture; a capture with no console file; a page with no accessibility snapshot; an inventory entry with no motion sample; no reduced-motion pass; no `DESIGN.md`; no `audit/`. Nothing at all: `all · no evidence`. The protocol: every finding names its evidence file beside the line; findings in impact order (blocks the task, wrong result, slower, cosmetic); one cause on many elements is one finding with the count; the removed lines are read (a state, a label, a reduced-motion branch or a token the diff removed is a finding at the line of the removal, its sentence starting `removed:`); the change is held to its criteria, never to more. **Motion stays inside `ui-ux`**: a separate gate would cost a `JudgementGateName`, a role, an agent description every session pays (finding 60), a second reading of the same images and a second ruling; the mechanical violations go to `design-lint` before any reviewer runs.

**The reviewer**, `agents/review-ui-ux.md`, receives the id, the two review paths, the criteria, `spec.md`, `review/ui-evidence.json` (read first), `review/ui/`, `review/design-lint.json`, the standard and `DESIGN.md` or `none`; it reads every image once and the JSON by grep; the three sections stay, the `read:` line gains `contract:` and `audit:`, each finding names its evidence file; no verdict line (the ruling is the judge's); sonnet, opus on the full route or a risk area, as today; `all · no evidence` replaces `all · no captures`; the description is rewritten at the same length.

**The judge** upholds a ui-ux defect only when its sentence names an evidence file that exists (or a line of the diff a capture cannot show, with the reason); a state, viewport or interaction the manifest lists as missing is a defect at the line of the capture spec; what the lint or axe already failed is an input, not a finding.

**The frontend lead** (`tech-lead.md`, the team list gains `frontend`) validates at the spec that every criterion on a page names its page, the states the diff adds or changes, its viewports and schemes and its motion thesis (or `none`), that a design contract exists or the item creates it or the user decided without one on record, and that the plan has the capture spec as a task; at the delivery that the manifest shows evidence for every declared state, viewport and interaction and the reduced-motion pass, that the ui-ux ruling is proven on the report's fingerprint and that the lint passed; the proof cited at `spec.md:<line>`, `review/ui-evidence.json:<line>`, `report.md:<line>`. The session is the frontend's primary lead already (`primaryTeam()`); the read-only `lead-frontend` seat validates the session's work as a stranger, as QA's does, and `evidence.ts` says `(the session; its lead validates)`.

**The method** for interface items: in `specify.md`, a criterion on a page carries in `spec.md` the lines `screen: /checkout · states: default, empty, error, success · viewports: 360, 768, 1280 · scheme: light, dark`, `motion: <what animates, its job, the value from the table> · rejected: <candidate, why>` or `motion: none`, and `design: DESIGN.md` or `design: none (D-n)`; the `--screen` flag is the record; the fast route takes the standard's defaults and no thesis. In `plan.md`, the capture spec (`tests/e2e/<page>.crew.spec.ts`, one `capture()` per declared state and viewport, one `captureMotion()` per interaction of the thesis) is a task of the plan, written red first; more than three pages make one todo per page. In `build.md`, the session reads `frontend.md` and `DESIGN.md` before the first interface file, writes no raw value where a token exists, gives every declared state its branch and its `capture()`, and animates only from the thesis with the table's values; `checklists/screen.md` is rewritten and `references/conclave.md` lists the kinds `motion` and `design-contract` the packs serve. In `gate.md`, rule 2 becomes "UI/UX needs evidence" (§4's last paragraph is its text). In `deliver.md`, the report's "UI evidence" section. `SKILL.md`'s Specify and Gate rows, its references table and rule 2 (never edit an evidence file under `review/ui/`, never hand-make a capture the fixture should take); `rules.md` rule 5's "Enforced by" and rule 9's `DESIGN.md`.

## 7. The frontend team and the paths (D4)

A design-contract path plans `design-lint`, `e2e` and the frontend seats.

## 8. The packs (D7)

## 9. The evals' sandbox and the new cases

The sandbox gets a browser through a shared fixture, `plugins/core/evals/fixtures/playwright-page.sh`: `@playwright/test`, `playwright` and `playwright-core` copied from the repository's `node_modules` (a devDependency of the root, tooling like Biome; the engine stays dependency-free) into the scaffold, a `playwright.config.mjs` with `channel: "chrome"` and `headless: true` (the machine's Chrome; no download, since `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH` is dropped by `kitEnvironment`), a `public/index.html` opened by `file://` (no port), the socketless environment of `env-observed-proof` extracted into `fixtures/flag-env.sh`, and a spec importing the crew's fixture by the plugin folder's absolute path. `SANDBOX_TAGS` gains `ui`.

## 10. Token cost per interface item (standard route)

The standard at about 1.5k tokens, read four times; the rubric 0.6k more; images at about width times height over 750 (1280×800 is 1.4k, 360×800 is 0.4k): a page with four states is about ten images, 9.5k; at most three pages (under 30k) and six motion strips (1.4k each); JSON summaries only (axe violations only, vitals under 300 bytes, the manifest under 2 KB, Lighthouse scores and failing audits only). About 45 to 60k tokens more per interface item, 0.15 to 0.25 USD on sonnet and twice that on the full route's opus; `fast-route-overhead` does not change.
